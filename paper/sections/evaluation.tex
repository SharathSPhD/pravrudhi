\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The first target is adapter-only self-improvement of a four-billion-parameter open model on a grade-school arithmetic benchmark with a deterministic answer checker. The train split supplies the proposer's data; the test split is sealed as the held-out pool from which the kernel draws rotations under an exposure cap; a disjoint slice of the train split is the in-loop evaluation set. Candidates are bucketed by edit family so that the hierarchical posterior has between-family variance. Before any candidate is selected, the unmodified model is evaluated across rotations and sampling seeds to measure the evaluation noise floor; this noise floor fixes the sequential boundaries and the surprise threshold. Claims are stated at the tier they passed: smoke, screen, or confirm. A confirm-tier claim requires at least three seeds, a paired design, a bias-corrected bootstrap interval on Hedges' $g$, and family-wise correction at family closure.

\subsection{External proof tiers}
\label{sec:eval-external}
The loop's own instrument, sealed pools with paired rotations and a sequential boundary, selects candidates; it does not by itself establish that a promotion is an improvement on a public benchmark. Two proof tiers are therefore scored by external tools with the same invocation before and after a loop, and the paper reports those numbers with the standard errors the tools compute. For the model track, \texttt{lm-evaluation-harness} scores GSM8K test (five-shot, exact match) for the base model and for the exported adapter. For the harness track, the fixed model runs under the initial harness and under the promoted harness on HumanEval+, a benchmark that never enters the loop, and EvalPlus's own evaluator executes the hidden tests; the loop itself uses MBPP+ with hidden tests executed inside a kernel-launched container without network. This is the shape of the harness self-improvement claims in the archive-based agent literature: a fixed model, a mutable scaffold, external deterministic scoring, held-out tasks.
